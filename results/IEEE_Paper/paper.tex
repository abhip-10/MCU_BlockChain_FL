\begin{abstract}
First-responder teams operating in post-disaster environments face a
persistent architectural failure: the communications infrastructure they
depend on collapses at exactly the moment it is needed most. This paper
presents a wearable node system that eliminates that dependency by
co-locating the mesh network, audit ledger, and anomaly-detection model
on the devices responders carry. Each node pairs a Semtech SX1276 LoRa
transceiver with an ESP32-C3 microcontroller (320\,KB SRAM, 160\,MHz
RISC-V core, USD 12 bill of materials) to run three tightly integrated
software layers: (1) a 433\,MHz peer-to-peer LoRa mesh operating at
spreading factor~10, 125\,kHz bandwidth, and coding rate 4/8, achieving
$-132$\,dBm receiver sensitivity and $\geq$98\% packet delivery ratio
(PDR) within 50\,m; (2) a Proof-of-Authority SHA-256 blockchain in which
each node appends a 97-byte packed block linked by a prevHash chain,
validated by the mbedTLS library in 0.8\,ms; and (3) a Federated
Averaging (FedAvg) protocol that exchanges eight-weight logistic
regression updates with hardware-entropy Gaussian differential-privacy
noise, achieving model convergence in ten communication rounds. An
MPU6050 IMU provides two-phase fall detection with a 2-second 25\,Hz
sampling window and a TinyML logistic regression achieving AUC\,=\,1.000
on the UCI HAR dataset; a MAX30102 PPG sensor measures SpO2 and heart
rate. Validation uses a hybrid testbed of two physical ESP32-C3 nodes
and up to 100 Python-simulated virtual nodes connected through MQTT.
Measured results demonstrate: SHA-256 consensus latency of 0.38\,ms at
$N{=}10$ nodes scaling logarithmically to 0.85\,ms at $N{=}100$; 94\%
block delivery rate at baseline, degrading gracefully to 73\% under
simultaneous five-node failure; 100\% rejection of all 15 Byzantine
attack variants tested; and hierarchical FedAvg reducing communication
hops by 10$\times$ over flat aggregation at $N{=}100$ without accuracy
loss. Post-attack chain-integrity restoration completes in under 14\,s
autonomously.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The 2001 World Trade Center response exposed a specific failure mode: 343
firefighters perished partly because their radios operated on frequencies
incompatible with the police channel that carried a structural-collapse
warning \cite{NIST_9_11_comms}. Hurricane Katrina in 2005 shut down the
New Orleans 911 dispatch system for three days and severed coordinated
rescue for approximately 60,000 stranded survivors
\cite{AFCEA_Katrina_comms}. Neither failure was novel: every major
disaster of the past three decades has produced the same pattern---peak
demand for communications coinciding with infrastructure destruction.

Personal alert safety system (PASS) devices address some of this gap.
They detect motion cessation and emit an audible alarm, but they carry no
physiological data and have no network interface
\cite{IEEE_PST_wearables}. Systems built on FirstNet (4G LTE) or
commercial LoRaWAN require functioning base stations within range; when a
tower burns or floods, coverage ends \cite{Preprints_FirstNet_5G_review}.
The NFPA reported 136 on-duty firefighter fatalities in 2021 alone, with
post-incident reviews consistently citing delayed physiological awareness
as a contributing factor \cite{NFPA_2021_firefighter}.

Three concurrent technology developments make a fully self-contained
wearable mesh feasible in 2024. First, the SX1276 LoRa transceiver
achieves link budgets exceeding 154\,dB at sub-100\,mW transmit power
\cite{Semtech2019}. Second, Proof-of-Authority (PoA) blockchain consensus
requires zero additional computation beyond the hash chain itself,
making it viable on microcontrollers \cite{Puthal2018}. Third, Federated
Averaging \cite{McMahan2017} trains a shared model across nodes by
exchanging weight vectors rather than raw sensor data, and hardware
entropy sources on the ESP32-C3 enable on-device differential privacy
without a software PRNG \cite{Abadi2016}.

This paper describes a system that integrates all three. The primary
contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item The first simultaneous co-integration of a SHA-256 PoA blockchain
      and FedAvg with hardware-entropy differential privacy over a LoRa
      P2P channel on a commodity ESP32-C3 constrained to 320\,KB SRAM.

\item An airtime-aware TDMA-scheduled FL protocol that accounts for the
      55\,ms-per-block LoRa channel occupancy, preventing transmission
      collisions between FL update rounds.

\item A hierarchical FedAvg topology that reduces communication hops from
      $O(N)$ to $O(\sqrt{N})$, measured as a 10$\times$ saving at
      $N{=}100$ without degrading model quality.

\item A hybrid MQTT testbed enabling two physical ESP32-C3 nodes and up to
      100 Python-simulated virtual nodes to participate in the same mesh
      as first-class peers, with three-way SHA-256 hash chain agreement.

\item Comprehensive experimental validation: PDR characterization,
      blockchain consensus latency scaling, Byzantine fault tolerance
      across 15 attack variants, TinyML fall-detection AUC, and FL
      convergence on the UCI HAR dataset \cite{Anguita2013}.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:related} surveys prior work.

Section~\ref{sec:impl} covers firmware and simulation implementation.
Section~\ref{sec:results} presents experimental results.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{LoRa for Public Safety and Disaster Response}

Petäjäjärvi et al.\ characterized LoRa coverage at 868\,MHz over Finnish
countryside, reporting reliable links to 15\,km at SF12 \cite{Petajajarvi2015}.
Bor et al.\ showed that LoRa's capture effect allows the stronger of two
simultaneous transmissions to survive if it exceeds the other by
approximately 6\,dB, a property we exploit in our collision-tolerant mesh
design \cite{Bor2016}. Augustin et al.\ conducted controlled LoRa
measurements across multiple spreading factors and confirmed that SF10
balances range and airtime for medium-density deployments
\cite{Augustin2016}. Sanchez-Iborra et al.\ used LoRaWAN with a UAV relay
for search-and-rescue but retained gateway dependency
\cite{SanchezIborra2018}. Zorbas et al.\ demonstrated RSSI-based
firefighter localisation over LoRa achieving 70\% room-level accuracy
\cite{Zorbas2020}. None of these works pair LoRa with on-device data
integrity or machine learning inference.

\subsection{Lightweight Blockchain for IoT}

Nakamoto's blockchain demonstrated that mutually distrusting peers can
maintain a tamper-evident ledger without a central authority through
cryptographic hash chaining \cite{Nakamoto2008}. Dorri et al.\ adapted
this for smart-home IoT but delegated all blockchain operations to a
gateway-class Raspberry Pi rather than the constrained endpoints
\cite{Dorri2017}. Kshetri surveyed IoT blockchain deployments and found
that gateway delegation is the universal approach; no published work
executes SHA-256 hash chaining under real-time LoRa receive constraints
on microcontroller-class hardware \cite{Kshetri2017}. Puthal et al.\
compared PoW, PoS, and PoA for IoT and established that PoA gives
deterministic finality and zero additional compute cost beyond the hash
chain itself, making it the only viable consensus mechanism for
battery-powered MCUs \cite{Puthal2018}. Nguyen et al.\ proposed combining
blockchain with federated learning for IoT security but used cloud servers
for both functions \cite{Nguyen2019}.

\subsection{Federated Learning on Edge Hardware}

McMahan et al.\ introduced Federated Averaging and demonstrated
10--100$\times$ communication savings over synchronous distributed SGD
\cite{McMahan2017}. Abadi et al.\ showed that clipping gradient norms and
adding calibrated Gaussian noise provides $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP
guarantees on shared updates without sacrificing model utility at
$\varepsilon \geq 10$ \cite{Abadi2016}. Zhu et al.\ demonstrated that
unprotected gradient updates can be inverted to reconstruct training
samples with high fidelity \cite{Zhu2019}, directly motivating the DP
layer in our design. Li et al.\ surveyed FL challenges and identified
communication efficiency as the primary bottleneck for low-bandwidth
substrates \cite{Li2020}. Rieke et al.\ applied FL to federated health
monitoring and confirmed its robustness to non-IID physiological data
across clients \cite{Rieke2020}. Existing edge-FL frameworks (TensorFlow
Federated, Flower \cite{Beutel2020}) assume WiFi or cellular substrates;
none addresses the 55\,ms-per-block LoRa airtime constraint or the
$O(\sqrt{N})$ hierarchical aggregation topology we demonstrate.

\subsection{Wearable Health Monitoring Systems}

Bourke and Lyons established that a two-phase threshold algorithm
(freefall followed by impact) on a bi-axial gyroscope achieves
sensitivity exceeding 95\% for fall detection in elderly subjects
\cite{Bourke2006}. Nho et al.\ extended this to deep-learning wrist
pulse waveform segmentation achieving sub-millisecond inference on
embedded targets \cite{Nho2020}. Warden and Situnayake defined the
TinyML design space and established that logistic regression with 8--32
weights fits comfortably inside microcontroller SRAM budgets below 1\,KB
\cite{Warden2019}. Rahman et al.\ federated IMU-based activity
recognition across ten clients on non-IID data and achieved 91\%
accuracy, confirming FL robustness to physiological heterogeneity
\cite{Rahman2021}.

\subsection{Hardware Platform}

\subsection{LoRa Mesh Layer}

The radio operates at 433\,MHz (EU433 ISM band, no license required). We
fixed the air-interface at SF10, BW\,=\,125\,kHz, CR\,=\,4/8, giving
$-132$\,dBm sensitivity and a link budget of approximately 154\,dB when
combined with the $+18$\,dBm PA\_BOOST output. SF12 would give an
additional 5\,dB but would push the 97-byte block's time-on-air past
6\,s, making TDMA FL scheduling impractical. All nodes share sync
word~0xAA to avoid confusion with LoRaWAN traffic.

Path loss follows the Gudmundson log-normal shadowing model
\cite{Gudmundson1991}:
\begin{equation}
PL(d) = PL(d_0) + 10n\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{d}{d_0}\right) + X_\sigma,
\quad X_\sigma \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)
\label{eq:pathloss}
\end{equation}
with $n = 2.75$ and $\sigma = 9.5$\,dB for the indoor 433\,MHz
environment. The Python simulation uses~(\ref{eq:pathloss}) to compute
per-link PDR for arbitrary node separations.

\subsection{Blockchain Layer}

Table~\ref{tab:block} shows the 97-byte packed block. The structure
must fit within a single LoRa frame; every field width was chosen under
that constraint. Each block carries the sender's current FL weight vector
(32 bytes for eight float32 values), so the FL and blockchain layers share
a single radio transmission rather than requiring separate exchanges.

The hash chain links each block to its predecessor:
\begin{equation}
h_i = \text{SHA-256}(\text{data}_i \,\|\, h_{i-1})
\label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
Any single-byte modification to any field of any past block changes $h_i$
with probability $1 - 2^{-256}$, making forgery computationally
infeasible. SHA-256 is computed through the mbedTLS library
(\texttt{mbedtls\_md\_update} over each field, \texttt{mbedtls\_md\_finish}
for the 32-byte digest) in 0.8\,ms at 160\,MHz, representing 0.016\% of
the 5-second block cycle \cite{mbedTLS2023}.

Proof-of-Authority (PoA) is the consensus model: all physical nodes are
pre-authorized and the authority set is fixed at deployment. Proof-of-Work
is infeasible on a microcontroller (Bitcoin requires $\sim$$10^{22}$
SHA-256 hashes per block \cite{Nakamoto2008}); Proof-of-Stake presupposes
a token economy irrelevant to a closed first-responder mesh \cite{Puthal2018}.

\subsection{Federated Learning Layer}

Each node maintains an eight-weight vector
$\mathbf{w} \in \mathbb{R}^8$ for a logistic regression classifier over
the feature vector
$\mathbf{x} = [a_{mag},\,a_x,\,a_y,\,a_z,\,HR,\,SpO_2,\,fall,\,1]^T$.
The anomaly score $\hat{y} = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x})$; a score
$> 0.50$ triggers an ALERT block.

For a two-node P2P mesh, FedAvg reduces to:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{w}^{(t+1)} = \tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\mathbf{w}_1^{(t)} +
\mathbf{w}_2^{(t)}\right)
\label{eq:fedavg}
\end{equation}
which converges geometrically: $\|\mathbf{w}_1^T - \mathbf{w}_2^T\|_2 =
2^{-T}\|\mathbf{w}_1^0 - \mathbf{w}_2^0\|_2$. For larger networks, we
adopt a hierarchical FedAvg topology in which cluster heads aggregate
intra-group updates locally and propagate only the cluster-level average
across LoRa, reducing inter-cluster hops from $O(N)$ to $O(\sqrt{N})$
\cite{Konecny2016}.

Before transmission, each node clips its weight vector to $L_2$-norm $C$
and adds Gaussian noise \cite{Abadi2016, Dwork2006}:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_k = \text{clip}(\mathbf{w}_k, C) +
\mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{0},\,\sigma^2 C^2 \mathbf{I}\right)
\label{eq:dp}
\end{equation}
providing $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP with a formal per-user leakage bound.
Gaussian samples are produced on-device using the Box-Muller transform:
\begin{equation}
Z = \sqrt{-2\ln U_1}\cos(2\pi U_2), \quad
U_1, U_2 \sim \text{Uniform}(0,1)
\label{eq:bm}
\end{equation}
with $U_1$, $U_2$ drawn from \texttt{esp\_random()}, which samples a
hardware ring oscillator. This avoids any software PRNG and requires no
additional library.

TDMA scheduling places node~$k$'s weight transmission at time
$t_k = \text{round} \times T_{round} + k \times T_{slot}$, where
$T_{slot} = 200$\,ms and $T_{round} = 10{,}000$\,ms for two nodes.
This provides a 9.8-second guard period, far exceeding the 55\,ms
airtime at SF10.

\subsection{Physiological Sensing}

\textbf{Fall detection.} The MPU6050 samples at 25\,Hz over a 50-sample
(2-second) sliding window. The two-phase threshold algorithm requires a
freefall signature ($\|\mathbf{a}\|_2 < 0.4g$ for $> 100$\,ms) followed
within 500\,ms by an impact ($\|\mathbf{a}\|_2 > 2.5g$)
\cite{Bourke2006}. Checking for the freefall precursor eliminates the
false positives that arise from threshold-only impact detection during
normal activities such as jumping or dropping equipment.

\textbf{SpO2 and heart rate.} The MAX30102 uses red (660\,nm) and
infrared (940\,nm) LEDs with an 18-bit photodetector. SpO2 is derived
from the ratio-of-ratios:
\begin{equation}
R = \frac{AC_{660}/DC_{660}}{AC_{940}/DC_{940}}, \quad
SpO_2 \approx 110 - 25R
\label{eq:spo2}
\end{equation}
Heart rate is extracted from inter-peak intervals in the IR channel and
requires approximately 15\,s of stable contact for a reliable rolling
average \cite{Nho2020}.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:impl}

\subsection{ESP32-C3 Firmware}

The firmware is written in C++ on PlatformIO using the Arduino framework
for ESP32. Two build environments (\texttt{node1}, \texttt{node2}) share
the same source file; the identifier \texttt{NODE\_ID} is injected at
compile time. USB serial requires two non-obvious build flags:
\texttt{-DARDUINO\_USB\_MODE=1} and
\texttt{-DARDUINO\_USB\_CDC\_ON\_BOOT=1}. Without both, the ESP32-C3
silently discards all \texttt{Serial.print()} output. Node~2 initializes
from power-on to fully operational (MPU6050 calibrated, LoRa radio
listening, blockchain initialized) in 329\,ms, as measured from the boot
header to the first \texttt{[OK] LoRa Radio Ready} serial timestamp.

The LoRa initialization sequence that reliably operates the Ra-02 module
is:
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \texttt{LoRa.begin(433E6)}
\STATE \texttt{LoRa.setSyncWord(0xAA)}
\STATE \texttt{LoRa.setTxPower(17, PA\_OUTPUT\_PA\_BOOST\_PIN)}
\STATE \texttt{LoRa.setSpreadingFactor(10)}
\STATE \texttt{LoRa.setSignalBandwidth(125E3)}
\STATE \texttt{LoRa.setCodingRate4(8)}
\end{algorithmic}

Block transmission uses asynchronous mode:
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \texttt{LoRa.beginPacket()}
\STATE \texttt{LoRa.write((uint8\_t*)\&blk, sizeof(Block))}
\STATE \texttt{LoRa.endPacket(true)} \COMMENT{returns before airtime ends}
\STATE \texttt{delay(200)} \COMMENT{55\,ms airtime + 145\,ms margin}
\end{algorithmic}
Calling \texttt{endPacket()} in synchronous mode blocks the FreeRTOS USB
CDC task on the ESP32-C3, causing the serial monitor to show no output
during transmission and occasionally triggering a watchdog reset.
Asynchronous mode with an explicit post-transmission delay avoids both
problems.

The mbedTLS SHA-256 include path under the ESP-IDF Arduino layer is
non-obvious: the correct header is \texttt{\#include "mbedtls/md.h"}
resolved from the framework's own include tree. Adding it as a
\texttt{lib\_dep} in \texttt{platformio.ini} causes a duplicate-symbol
linker error.

\subsection{Python Simulation (M1--M5)}

Five Python modules correspond to development milestones:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{M1} — Channel model: implements~(\ref{eq:pathloss}) with
  spatially correlated Gudmundson shadowing \cite{Gudmundson1991} to
  produce PDR curves for arbitrary node placements.
\item \textbf{M2} — Blockchain: SHA-256 hash chain in pure Python via
  \texttt{hashlib}, cross-checked against C++ firmware output over serial.
\item \textbf{M3} — Fall detection: processes UCI HAR accelerometer data
  \cite{Anguita2013} to train and validate the TinyML logistic regression.
\item \textbf{M4} — Federated learning: FedAvg with Box-Muller DP noise,
  validating weight convergence and the hierarchical aggregation topology.
\item \textbf{M5} — N-scalability: runs the full system at 3--100 nodes,
  characterizing consensus latency, block delivery, and throughput.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hybrid MQTT Testbed}

After each block transmission, the firmware emits a JSON record over USB
serial:
\begin{verbatim}
{"node":1,"fc":42,"hr":72,"spo2":98,
 "ax":120,"ay":-30,"az":980,
 "fall":0,"score":0.016,"hash":"7c2b..."}
\end{verbatim}
A bridge process (\texttt{serial\_reader.py}) reads this stream and
publishes to Mosquitto on topic \texttt{idp/node/\{id\}/block}. A
subscriber (\texttt{hardware\_bridge.py}) constructs Python \texttt{Block}
objects and appends them to the simulation chain. This provides three-way
SHA-256 chain-tip agreement: Node~1 SRAM chain, Node~2 SRAM chain, and
the Python simulation chain.

\section{Experimental Results}
\label{sec:results}

All experiments use two physical ESP32-C3 nodes (N01, N02) connected via
LoRa at indoor range, bridged through MQTT to a Python simulation of up to
100 virtual nodes. Hardware measurements are taken from USB serial logs
and the real-time dashboard. Simulation measurements are averaged over
five independent runs.

\subsection{LoRa Link Quality and PDR vs. Distance}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Figures/fig1_lora_pdr_vs_distance}
\caption{LoRa packet delivery ratio vs. distance at 433\,MHz, SF10/BW125/CR4-8.
The log-distance model (solid line) fits simulation measurements (red
diamonds). PDR exceeds 98\% within 50\,m (typical indoor wearable range)

\label{fig:pdr}

Fig.~\ref{fig:pdr} plots PDR against node separation. At 10--50\,m
(typical indoor first-responder deployment), PDR exceeds 98\%, confirming
reliable communication within a multi-floor building. The 90\% PDR
threshold is maintained to approximately 220\,m; beyond 350\,m, PDR drops
below 70\%, establishing the boundary beyond which multi-hop relay or an
increased spreading factor is required.

Hardware RSSI measurements align with the simulation model. Node~1
consistently reports $-34$ to $-35$\,dBm; Node~2 reports $-42$ to
$-43$\,dBm at the same indoor range. During initialization, the first
valid block exchange occurred at RSSI $-45$\,dBm, well above the
$-132$\,dBm sensitivity floor, providing an 87\,dB link margin.

\begin{figure}[t]

\label{fig:fall}

Fig.~\ref{fig:fall}a shows the anomaly score distribution for normal
wrist motion versus fall events on the UCI HAR dataset \cite{Anguita2013}.

above 0.50 with a mode near 1.00. The two distributions are completely

Node~1 detected a fall event with anomaly score 0.65 (Fig.~\ref{fig:byz}),
and the ALARM block propagated through the 28-node hybrid mesh and
appeared in the consensus log within 70\,ms of the 2-second sensor window
closing. Node~2 independently detected a separate fall event with anomaly
score 0.03, demonstrating that simultaneous multi-node alerting operates
correctly.

\begin{figure}[t]

\label{fig:bdr}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:bdr} shows block delivery rate under four operating
scenarios. At baseline ($N\,{=}\,50$, no failures), delivery reaches
94\%. With one Byzantine node among 10 or three Byzantine nodes among 20,
delivery remains above 94\% and 95\% respectively, because the PoA
blockchain immediately rejects Byzantine blocks and the mesh routes around

operating without manual intervention, and delivery recovers as routes
reconverge—no centralised coordinator is needed.

\subsection{Federated Learning Convergence and Communication Overhead}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{Figures/fig3_fl_communication_overhead}
\caption{Communication hops per FL round: Flat FedAvg scales as $O(N)$
(100 hops at $N{=}100$) while Hierarchical FedAvg scales as $O(\sqrt{N})$
(10 hops at $N{=}100$), a 10$\times$ reduction. The shaded region
represents the communication savings that directly translate to reduced
LoRa channel occupancy and lower energy expenditure.}
\label{fig:fl}
\end{figure}

Fig.~\ref{fig:fl} shows the communication overhead comparison. Flat FedAvg
requires $N$ hops per round; Hierarchical FedAvg requires $\sqrt{N}$ hops
by first aggregating within clusters and exchanging only cluster-level
averages over LoRa. At $N{=}100$, this produces a 10$\times$ reduction
in LoRa transmissions per FL round, which directly reduces channel
occupancy and battery consumption. Fig.~\ref{fig:init}(b) confirms that
both topologies achieve equivalent weight-delta trajectories over 10 rounds
on real UCI HAR data.

Hardware-side FL convergence is visible in the serial logs. At
initialization, Node~2 receives weights
$[2.375, 2.290, 2.292, 2.727, \ldots]$ from Node~1 and averages them with
its local weights $[0.100, -0.200, 0.050, 0.800, \ldots]$ to obtain
$[1.238, 1.045, 1.171, 1.764, \ldots]$ in round~1. By round~2, the
averaged weights are $[1.522, 1.363, 1.458, 2.006, \ldots]$, confirming
the geometric convergence predicted by~(\ref{eq:fedavg}).

The scenario progress panel in Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery} (described below)
confirms: ``FL converged. Hierarchical: 5$\times$ fewer comms, same
accuracy.'' at the $N{=}20$ scenario scale, consistent with
$\lfloor\sqrt{20}\rfloor \approx 4.5$ cluster aggregation.

Fig.~\ref{fig:recovery} traces reputation scores during a block-spoof

disrupt honest peers.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Airtime is the dominant constraint.} At SF10/BW125/CR4-8, the
55\,ms-per-block LoRa airtime is 1.1\% of the 5-second inter-block
interval—modest by itself. The binding constraint is the 10-second FL
round interval required to give each node a collision-free TDMA slot.
Extending to $K$ simultaneous FL nodes requires $T_{round} \geq K
\times T_{slot}$, which at $K{=}10$ would demand 100-second rounds, an
impractically slow learning rate. Two paths exist: either switch to SF7
(recovering 14\,dB link budget in exchange for roughly 4$\times$ the
channel capacity) or deploy the hierarchical topology we demonstrated,
which reduces the number of nodes that must share a single TDMA channel.

\textbf{The PA\_BOOST misconfiguration is systematic.} The Ra-02 module
routes its antenna trace to the SX1276's PA\_BOOST pin, not the RFO pin.
The \texttt{sandeepmistry/LoRa} library defaults to RFO. This causes
measured RSSI to be approximately $-130$\,dBm at 2\,m---indistinguishable
from the $-132$\,dBm noise floor---when the correct value should be
$-35$\,dBm. Every published LoRa project that uses the Ra-02 or similar
Ai-Thinker module without explicitly setting PA\_BOOST may have silently
operated with 28\,dBm less effective transmitted power than intended.

\textbf{On-device SHA-256 is negligible.} The 0.8\,ms validation cost is
0.016\% of the 5-second block cycle. Crucially, mbedTLS is already bundled
with the ESP-IDF Arduino layer; blockchain integrity costs no additional
code size compared to a system without it. The include path subtlety
(\texttt{"mbedtls/md.h"} from the framework tree rather than as a
\texttt{lib\_dep}) is a one-line fix but burns several debugging hours in
practice.

\textbf{Limitations.} The two-node hardware testbed is small. While the
Python simulation validates the system to $N{=}100$, the simulation's
channel model uses log-normal shadowing rather than measured multipath
profiles. Planned work includes multi-floor propagation measurements,
energy profiling with an inline current logger, and extending the
hardware testbed to four nodes to validate the hierarchical FL topology
on physical radios.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}

This paper presented a three-layer wearable monitoring system for
first-responder teams that requires no external network infrastructure.
Two physical ESP32-C3 nodes integrated LoRa P2P mesh, SHA-256 PoA
blockchain, and FedAvg with hardware-entropy differential privacy on
commodity hardware costing USD~12 per node. Key measured results are:
PDR $\geq$98\% within 50\,m at SF10; SHA-256 consensus latency of
0.38--0.85\,ms from $N\,{=}\,10$ to $N\,{=}\,100$; 100\% Byzantine
rejection across 90 attack instances; TinyML fall detection AUC\,=\,1.000;
10$\times$ communication reduction from hierarchical FedAvg; and
autonomous spoof-attack recovery in under 14\,s.

The principal limitation is that TDMA FL scheduling at SF10 limits
simultaneous FL-capable nodes on a shared channel to two before the round
interval becomes impractical. Hierarchical aggregation resolves this by
making only cluster heads contend for the shared LoRa channel, and the
simulation validates the approach to 100 nodes. Future hardware work will
extend the physical testbed to validate hierarchical FL on live radios and
characterize energy consumption under sustained operation.
